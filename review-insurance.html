Annual Insurance Review

Your policies were sized for the life you had when you bought them. Houses get renovated, cars get added, kids arrive, premiums quietly creep — and none of it updates itself. Once a year, spend an hour or two making sure your coverage still matches your actual life.

🗓️ Once a year, every year ⏱️ 1–2 hours for most households 💸 Loyalty tax: $300–900/yr for not shopping ☂️ Covers home · auto · life · umbrella

Why it matters

Insurance fails quietly. Nothing looks wrong until the day you file a claim — and by then the gap is unfixable. An annual review catches the drift while it's still free to correct (figures below are typical U.S. estimates):

Rebuild costs drift up

Construction and material costs rise every year while your coverage limit sits where you set it. The shortfall only shows itself at claim time — when it's too late to add coverage.

The loyalty tax

Renewal premiums tend to creep up year after year for customers who never shop around. Comparison quotes once a year are how you find out what the market actually charges.

Life changes outdate policies

Marriage, a new baby, a renovation, a teen driver, a home business — each one changes what you need covered, and none of them notifies your insurer for you.

Silent coverage gaps

Beneficiaries go stale

Life insurance pays whoever is named on the form — not who you'd pick today. Ex-spouses and deceased relatives stay listed until you change them. Two minutes to check, devastating to get wrong.

    📌 Make it stick: anchor your review to a date you can't forget — your renewal month, tax season (the paperwork's already out), or your birthday. A yearly task with no anchor is a task that quietly stops happening.

    The cost of skipping it

    The review itself costs you an hour or two and zero dollars. Here's what skipping it tends to cost instead (typical U.S. estimates):

    🗓️ The annual review itself1–2 hours, $0
    💸 Loyalty tax from never shopping$300–900 every year
    🏚️ Underinsured major claim (rebuild shortfall)$10,000–100,000+
    And the quietest one isn't on the chart: outdated beneficiaries. Updating them today costs nothing and takes minutes. Discovered after a death, it's unfixable — the money goes where the old form says, not where you'd want it to. That asymmetry is the whole argument for a yearly look.

    How to run your review

    Set aside an evening, pull the paperwork, and work through the checklist — your progress saves automatically, so you can spread it over a few days if quotes take time to come back.

    ⚠️ Two rules before you change anything

    • Never cancel a policy until its replacement is ACTIVE — confirmed in writing, with the effective date in hand. Even a one-day gap in coverage can be expensive and can raise your future rates.
    • Record a quick video walkthrough of your home while you're at it — narrate as you pan each room, open closets, film serial numbers on big-ticket items. Ten minutes of footage is enormous leverage at claim time, when you'd otherwise be reconstructing your belongings from memory.

    When to talk to a professional

    • An independent agent can compare across carriers in one conversation — unlike a captive agent, who represents a single company. For the shopping step, that's a real shortcut.
    • After a major life event: marriage, divorce, a new child, a death in the family, retirement, or an inheritance. These change both what you need covered and who should benefit.
    • If you run a business or own significant assets. Home-based businesses, rental properties, and growing net worth all raise questions a standard personal policy wasn't written to answer.
    • If anything in your policy reads like a foreign language. Exclusions, endorsements, sub-limits — a licensed agent explains them for free. Never skip coverage just because you didn't understand the clause.

    Common questions

    What's the difference between actual cash value and replacement cost?

    Replacement cost pays what it takes to buy the item new today; actual cash value pays that amount minus depreciation. For a five-year-old couch or roof, the difference can be most of the check. Your declarations page says which one each policy uses — it's one of the most important lines to find during your review.

    What exactly is an umbrella policy?

    Extra liability coverage that kicks in after your auto or home liability limits are exhausted — one policy spanning above both. It exists for the rare-but-huge events: a serious at-fault accident or an injury lawsuit. Typically sold in $1 million increments and generally inexpensive relative to the coverage, because it's rarely triggered.

    Will shopping for quotes hurt my credit score?

    No. Insurance quotes use a "soft pull" of your credit, which doesn't affect your score no matter how many quotes you collect. This is different from applying for a loan or credit card. Shop as widely as you like.

    How much can switching actually save?

    It varies a lot, but people who haven't shopped in several years commonly find differences of a few hundred dollars per year on the same coverage — that's the "loyalty tax" in action. Even if you don't switch, a competing quote is useful leverage when you call your current carrier. Just compare like for like: same limits, same deductibles.

    Is renters insurance really worth it?

    Usually, yes — it's typically among the cheapest policies there is (often $15–30/month, U.S. typical), and it covers two things people forget: your belongings (your landlord's policy covers the building, not your stuff) and personal liability if someone is hurt in your home. Worth a fresh look at your annual review even if you skipped it when you moved in.
