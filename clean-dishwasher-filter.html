Clean Your Dishwasher Filter

Hiding under the bottom rack of almost every modern dishwasher is a twist-out filter — and it's been quietly collecting every crumb you didn't scrape off. Five minutes a month keeps your dishes actually clean, your kitchen smelling fine, and your pump alive.

🗓️ Typically once a month ⏱️ ~5 minutes, no tools required 🧼 Just dish soap + a soft brush 💸 Skipping it risks $150–$400 pump repairs

Why it matters

Your dishwasher doesn't grind food away — it catches it in a filter and reuses the wash water. When that filter clogs, every problem below starts with the same five minutes you skipped (costs are typical U.S. estimates):

Grit on "clean" dishes

A full filter means the machine recirculates dirty water — redepositing fine food particles onto everything in the load. If plates come out with sandy specks, this is why.

Every load, re-washed

Sour, swampy smells

Trapped food rots in warm, damp darkness between cycles. That "dishwasher smell" that greets you when you open the door is almost always the filter, not the machine.

Free to fix — today

Standing water & poor draining

The filter sits over the drain. Clog it and you get a puddle in the tub after every cycle — the first step on the road to a "broken dishwasher" service call.

$150 – $250 service call

Pump & spray-arm strain

Debris that escapes a packed filter ends up in the circulation pump and spray-arm jets. Pumps wear out fast pushing chunky water — and they're the priciest part in the machine.

$150 – $400 pump replacement

Cloudy glassware

Recirculated grease and grit film onto glass first. If your glasses look hazier every month, clean the filter before you blame the detergent — or buy new glasses.

$30 – $100 in "fixes"

How often should you clean it?

Once a month is the rule of thumb, but the right interval depends on how hard your dishwasher works and what you feed it. Answer three questions for a personalized schedule:

1. How many loads do you run?
2. What happens to plates before they go in?
3. Does your machine have a manual filter or a built-in grinder?

    How to clean your dishwasher filter

    The whole job takes about five minutes at the kitchen sink. Work through the checklist — your progress saves automatically, so you can pick it back up if you get interrupted.

    ⚠️ Before you start — three things worth knowing

    • Find your filter first. Check your manual (or search your model number + "filter") — on most machines it's a round, twist-out cylinder in the tub floor, usually under the bottom spray arm or tucked in a back corner.
    • Never run the dishwasher without the filter seated. An unfiltered cycle sends debris straight into the pump — the exact thing the filter exists to prevent.
    • No harsh abrasives. Steel wool and stiff bristles can tear the fine mesh, and a torn filter is a useless filter. Soft brush, warm water, dish soap — that's the whole kit.

    🧰 What you'll need

    Soft brush or old toothbrush Dish soap Towel

    📐 Three things worth seeing first

    Locate, then twist it out

    The filter is a round cylinder in the tub floor. A quarter-turn counterclockwise unlocks it; lift it straight up and out. No tools, no force — if it won't turn, check the arrows printed on it.

    Two parts, rinsed separately

    Most filters separate into a coarse outer cage (catches big debris — rinse and shake) and a fine mesh cylinder (catches grease and grit — warm water, dish soap, soft brush only).

    Check the spray arms too

    While the rack is out, spin each spray arm and eyeball the jet holes. Seeds, rice, and label scraps lodge in them — pop blockages out with a toothpick and the wash improves instantly.

    When to call for service

    • Water still won't drain after a filter clean. The clog is likely deeper — in the drain hose, the air gap, or a kinked line under the sink. Worth a pro if a basic hose check doesn't find it.
    • Grinding, rattling, or whining noises during the wash cycle. That's usually something hard (glass shard, fruit pit) in the pump, or a pump bearing on its way out.
    • Error codes that come back after you've cleaned the filter and power-cycled the machine. Drain-related codes (like LE, OE, or E24 depending on brand) that persist mean a sensor or pump issue.
    • Leaks. Water on the kitchen floor is never a filter problem — door gaskets, hoses, and valves are pro territory, and fast action saves the cabinetry.
    • Typical diagnostic visits run $100–$150, often credited toward the repair. Mention that you've already cleaned the filter — it's the first thing they'd check.

    Common questions

    How do I know if my dishwasher has a manual filter?

    Pull out the bottom rack and look at the tub floor. A round plastic cylinder or grid you can grip and twist — usually with little arrows printed on it — is a manual filter, and nearly every machine made after about 2010 has one. If the floor is flat with no removable parts and your machine is older and noticeably loud, it may have a self-cleaning grinder instead. The manual (or your model number plus "filter" in a search) settles it in 30 seconds.

    My dishwasher smells even after I cleaned the filter. Now what?

    Wipe the sump area where the filter sits, the door gasket (fold it back — gunk hides in the crease), and the bottom inside edge of the door, which the spray never reaches. Then run an empty hot cycle with a bowl of white vinegar on the top rack. If a smell survives all that, check the drain hose for a low sag where water sits.

    Does rinse aid have anything to do with the filter?

    Indirectly, yes. Rinse aid helps water sheet off dishes so grease and residue head for the drain instead of redepositing — which means a bit less film ending up on the fine mesh. It's no substitute for cleaning the filter, but the two together are why some dishwashers stay fresh for years.

    How do I run a vinegar cycle, exactly?

    Put one to two cups of white vinegar in a dishwasher-safe bowl, set it upright on the top rack of an otherwise empty machine, and run the hottest normal cycle. The vinegar disperses through the wash water, dissolving grease film and mineral buildup. Once a month after a filter clean is plenty — and skip the urge to mix in bleach, ever.

    Why are my glasses cloudy — is that the filter?

    Do the vinegar test: soak a cloudy glass in white vinegar for five minutes. If it clears, the haze is hard-water mineral film — a filter clean, a vinegar cycle, and rinse aid will fix it. If it stays cloudy, that's etching: permanent micro-scratching from soft water, too much detergent, or very hot cycles. Etching can't be reversed, but easing up on detergent prevents it on the next set.
