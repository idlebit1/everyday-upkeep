Gutter Upkeep

Gutters have one job: move rainwater away from your house. When they clog, that water goes into your fascia, your siding, your basement, and your foundation instead. Here's how often to clean them, when, why — and exactly how.

🗓️ Typically 2× a year ⏱️ 1–3 hours per cleaning 🧤 DIY-friendly on one-story homes 💸 Skipping it risks $4,000+ repairs

Why it matters

A clogged gutter doesn't just overflow — it concentrates hundreds of gallons of roof runoff in exactly the wrong places. These are the repairs that clean gutters prevent (typical U.S. cost ranges):

Foundation damage

Water pooling at the base of your walls erodes soil, then cracks and shifts the foundation. The single most expensive consequence of neglected gutters.

$4,000 – $12,000+

Basement flooding

Overflow runs straight down the wall and into the basement or crawl space, bringing mold and ruined flooring with it.

$2,000 – $7,000

Fascia & soffit rot

Standing water in the gutter wicks into the wooden boards it's attached to. Rotten fascia also means the gutter itself starts pulling away.

$600 – $2,500

Ice dams

In cold climates, clogged gutters trap water that freezes, backs up under shingles, and leaks into ceilings and walls.

$1,500 – $5,000

Pests

Wet leaf muck is prime real estate for mosquitoes, wasps, carpenter ants, and birds — right at your roofline.

$150 – $800 removal

Landscape erosion

A waterfall off the gutter edge trenches your beds, splashes mud on siding, and drowns plants below.

$300 – $1,500

    How to clean your gutters

    A full cleaning on an average single-story home takes 1–3 hours. Work through the checklist — your progress saves automatically, so you can pick it back up if you take a break.

    ⚠️ Safety first — ladders hurt more people than gutters ever will

    • Use a sturdy ladder on firm, level ground. A ladder standoff (stabilizer) is the best $40 you'll spend.
    • Keep three points of contact and never stand on the top two rungs.
    • Follow the belt-buckle rule: keep your hips between the rails. Move the ladder instead of reaching.
    • Have someone home who knows you're on a ladder. Skip windy or wet days.
    • Two stories or steep terrain? Strongly consider hiring it out. Pro cleaning costs far less than an ER visit.

    🧰 What you'll need

    Sturdy ladder + standoff Work gloves Safety glasses Gutter scoop or trowel Bucket with hook (or tarp below) Garden hose w/ spray nozzle Gutter sealant (for leaks) Screwdriver / drill (loose hangers)

    📐 Three techniques worth knowing

    The 4-to-1 ladder rule

    For every 4 feet of height, set the base 1 foot from the wall. A standoff (orange) braces against the roof — never against the gutter.

    Scoop away from the outlet

    Start beside the downspout and work outward, so debris never gets packed into the opening you're trying to keep clear.

    Send water 4–6 ft out

    Downspout extensions are the cheapest foundation insurance there is — about $10 and ten minutes per corner.

    When to call a pro instead

    • Two or more stories. Fall risk goes up dramatically; pros have the equipment.
    • Steep roof or sloped/uneven ground where a ladder can't sit safely.
    • Signs of bigger problems: sagging runs, separated seams, gutters pulling off the fascia, or visible fascia rot. Cleaning won't fix those.
    • Persistent downspout clogs that flushing and a drain auger can't clear (often underground drain lines).
    • Typical pro cleaning runs $120–$250 for an average home — many services include a basic inspection and minor tightening.

    Common questions

    Do gutter guards mean I never have to clean?

    No — they're a "less often," not a "never." Fine debris (shingle grit, seeds, pine needles) still gets through or mats on top. Plan on at least one inspection-and-clean a year, and check that water actually flows through the guards during a heavy rain.

    What's the single best time of year to clean gutters?

    Late fall, after the trees around you have finished dropping leaves but before the first hard freeze. That one cleaning prevents winter's worst problems: ice dams and frozen, clogged downspouts.

    How do I know my gutters are clogged without climbing up?

    Watch during a decent rain: water sheeting over the edges, a downspout that's barely trickling, or a "waterfall" at one corner all mean clogs. After rain, sagging sections and plants sprouting from the gutter are dead giveaways.

    How far should downspouts carry water from the house?

    At least 4–6 feet from the foundation. If your downspouts just dump at the base of the wall, adding extensions or splash blocks is a 10-minute, $10 fix with foundation-sized payoff.

    Can I clean gutters from the ground?

    Partially. Hose attachments and gutter-cleaning wands can flush light debris and are great between full cleanings, but they can't remove packed, composted muck — and you can't inspect what you can't see.
